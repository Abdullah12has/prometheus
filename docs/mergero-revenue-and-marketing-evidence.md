# Mergero Revenue and Marketing Evidence Dossier

**Research date:** 26 September 2026

**Purpose:** Give the project team a source-backed explanation of how an M&A advisory firm scales revenue, why marketing belongs beside outreach in the proposed GTM engine, what forms of marketing are relevant to Mergero, and where the evidence has limitations.

## 1. Executive conclusion

Mergero should not treat marketing as a replacement for owner outreach. It should use marketing as an **origination multiplier**:

1. Build and maintain a broad company universe.
2. Convert aggregated company and buyer-demand data into useful owner-facing insights.
3. Use those insights to establish trust and give owners a reason to engage.
4. Combine engagement signals with company fit to prioritize outreach.
5. Continue automated or adviser-led regional outreach.
6. Convert explicit owner interest into qualified mandates and MGX opportunities.

The commercial reasoning is straightforward. Advisory revenue is driven principally by completed assignments and average fees. Marketing can influence the number of qualified conversations, mandates won, referrals, pricing confidence and future pipeline. It does not itself complete an M&A transaction.

## 2. Evidence standards

This dossier distinguishes among:

- **Verified public fact:** Directly supported by the linked source.
- **Mergero-reported fact:** Published by Mergero but not independently audited here.
- **External research:** A survey, filing or dataset produced by another organization.
- **Inference:** A conclusion drawn from multiple facts and identified as such.
- **Recommendation:** A proposed action, not a proven Mergero result.
- **Illustration:** Hypothetical arithmetic that must not be treated as a forecast.

## 3. How an M&A advisory firm scales revenue

### 3.1 Revenue mechanics

A useful simplified model is:

```text
Advisory revenue
= completed mandates × average completion fee
+ retainers and progress fees
+ other advisory or recurring revenue
```

This is a simplified operating model, not Mergero's disclosed revenue formula.

### 3.2 Public-company evidence

Houlihan Lokey states that:

- Corporate Finance revenue primarily consists of fees from M&A, capital-markets and other corporate-finance advisory engagements.
- The majority of Corporate Finance revenue consists of completion fees.
- When a transaction fails to complete, fees are generally limited to retainers and sometimes progress fees.
- In the year ended 31 March 2024, Corporate Finance revenue fell 2% while completed transactions fell 11%; a higher average transaction fee partly offset the lower number of completions.

**What this supports:** Completed transaction volume and average fee are direct revenue levers for an M&A adviser.

**Source:** [Houlihan Lokey FY2024 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1302215/000130221524000087/hlify2410-k.pdf)

Houlihan Lokey's FY2026 filing again states that most Corporate Finance revenue consists of completion fees and reports Corporate Finance revenue of approximately $1.745 billion in FY2026, compared with $1.527 billion in FY2025 and $1.107 billion in FY2024.

**What this supports:** Transaction advisory revenue can grow materially when a firm increases successful activity and/or average fees, although a public global adviser is not directly comparable to Mergero.

**Source:** [Houlihan Lokey FY2026 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1302215/000130221526000053/hli-20260331.htm)

Lazard reported that:

- 2024 Financial Advisory adjusted net revenue increased 28% from the prior year.
- Revenue per managing director reached $8.6 million.
- The company connected this performance to improving M&A activity, client delivery and execution of its growth strategy.

**What this supports:** Adviser productivity, market activity and successful execution are important scaling variables alongside pure headcount growth.

**Source:** [Lazard 2024 Annual Letter to Shareholders](https://www.lazard.com/news-announcements/2024-annual-letter-to-shareholders/)

### 3.3 Practical revenue levers for a firm like Mergero

| Revenue lever | Commercial mechanism | Marketing contribution |
|---|---|---|
| More sell-side mandates | More owners appoint Mergero | High: awareness, trust, education, outreach support and referrals |
| More buy-side mandates | More acquirers retain Mergero | High: buyer-demand insights, proof and relationship marketing |
| Higher completion rate | More mandates reach a fee event | Moderate: better qualification and owner preparation; execution remains decisive |
| Higher average fee | Larger or more complex assignments and stronger pricing | Moderate: expertise and positioning can support perceived value |
| More revenue per adviser | More qualified opportunities handled with the same team | Indirect: data, automation and content reuse reduce repetitive origination work |
| More repeat and referred business | Existing relationships produce future work | High: systematic nurture, proof and partner enablement |

The contribution ratings are recommendations based on the cited revenue mechanics and marketing research. They are not measured Mergero attribution data.

## 5. Evidence that thought leadership can support commercial outcomes

### 5.1 2024 Edelman–LinkedIn study

Edelman and LinkedIn surveyed 3,484 management-level professionals across seven countries in late 2023. The audience included business decision-makers and owners, partners, founders and C-suite executives. Germany was one of the surveyed markets.

Reported findings include:

- 52% of decision-makers and 54% of C-suite executives spent at least one hour per week consuming thought leadership.
- 73% of decision-makers agreed that thought leadership is a more trustworthy basis for assessing capability than marketing materials and product sheets.
- More than 75% of decision-makers and C-suite executives said a piece of thought leadership had led them to research a product or service they were not previously considering.
- Nine in ten decision-makers and C-suite executives said they were moderately or very likely to be more receptive to outreach from a company that consistently produces high-quality thought leadership.
- 86% of decision-makers said they would be moderately or very likely to invite such a company into an RFP process.
- Among respondents who said thought leadership prompted them to research a previously unconsidered product or service, 23% said they began buying from or working with the organization that produced it.
- 60% agreed that good thought leadership made them more willing to pay a premium.

**What this supports:** Useful expertise can improve trust, receptivity to outreach, consideration and perceived value.

**What it does not prove:** It does not establish a specific conversion lift for Mergero, M&A advisory or small-business owners in the Nordics and DACH.

**Source:** [2024 Edelman–LinkedIn B2B Thought Leadership Impact Report](https://www.edelman.com/sites/g/files/aatuss191/files/2024-02/_2024%20Edelman-LinkedIn%20B2B%20Thought%20Leadership%20Impact%20Report%20Final.pdf?previewMode=true)

### 5.2 The 95–5 principle

LinkedIn's B2B Institute, citing research with the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, argues that only a small share of potential B2B customers is actively in market during a given period. Marketing to the larger out-of-market population is intended to build memory and availability for a later buying occasion, rather than generate immediate conversion.

**What this supports:** A long-term nurture strategy is directionally appropriate where buying occasions are infrequent.

**Relevance to Mergero:** A business owner may remain outside the M&A market for years before succession, an unsolicited buyer approach, strategic pressure or a personal event changes the timing.

**Limitation:** The 95–5 ratio is a general B2B heuristic, not a measured percentage of owners considering an M&A transaction.

**Source:** [LinkedIn B2B Institute: The 95–5 Rule](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/b2b-research/trends/95-5-rule)

### 5.3 Professional-services referral evidence

Hinge reports that 69% of buyers in its professional-services study were very willing to recommend their provider. It also reports that many referrals were not made because nobody asked.

**What this supports:** Referral generation can be systematized rather than left entirely to chance.

**Limitation:** The underlying study spans professional services and is not limited to M&A advisory.

**Source:** [Hinge: How Buyers Buy Professional Services](https://hingemarketing.com/library/article/new_study_highlights_how_buyers_buy_professional_services)

## 6. Evidence from adjacent M&A businesses

### 6.1 Axial's data-to-content model

Axial publishes an anonymized Pursuits Report based on actual activity on its lower-middle-market platform. Its first-half 2026 report states that:

- The data reflects buyer pursuit activity rather than only survey opinion.
- It is designed to help owners, advisers and investors understand where buyer attention is concentrated.
- The dataset anonymizes company descriptions and groups financial information into bands.
- The $1 million–$5 million EBITDA segment represented approximately 72% of buyer pursuits in the reported dataset.

**What this supports:** Proprietary platform activity can be converted into credible, confidentiality-preserving market content for owners and deal professionals.

**Source:** [Axial Pursuits Report: 1H 2026](https://www.axial.net/forum/axial-pursuits-report-1h-2026/)

### 6.2 Lazard's research practice

Lazard publishes M&A reviews, outlooks and private-market reports as part of its public insights program.

**What this supports:** A major financial adviser uses research and market interpretation as a visible element of its expertise and brand.

**What it does not prove:** Public availability of research does not by itself disclose how many mandates or how much revenue the content generates.

**Sources:** [Lazard 2025 M&A Review and 2026 Outlook](https://lazard.com/research-insights/lazard-2025-ma-review-and-2026-outlook/), [Lazard 2025 Secondary Market Report](https://lazard.com/research-insights/lazard-2025-secondary-market-report/)

### 6.3 Mergero's transaction evidence

Mergero publishes selected transactions across multiple sectors. Transaction announcements and case studies can act as proof of execution, industry familiarity and buyer access.

**Source:** [Mergero selected transactions](https://mergero.com/deals/)

**Recommendation:** Convert each permitted transaction into an owner-relevant lesson, not only an announcement. Possible angles include preparation, strategic rationale, cross-border buyer access, confidentiality and factors that improved execution.

## 7. Recommended marketing system for Mergero

This section is a product direction derived from the evidence, not a report of current Mergero operations.

### 7.1 Three content families

1. **Market benchmarks**
   - Sector, size and geographic comparisons.
   - Aggregated transaction-readiness characteristics.
   - Trends in revenue quality, management depth and buyer attention.

2. **Buyer-demand briefs**
   - Characteristics active buyers are seeking.
   - Sectors and business models receiving repeated demand.
   - Confidential buyer identities and mandates excluded unless disclosure is approved.

3. **Exit-readiness education**
   - Financial, legal, management and operational preparation.
   - Succession planning and timing.
   - Common reasons transactions stall.

### 7.2 Account-specific activation

The GTM engine should select the most relevant approved asset for each company and generate a safe introduction grounded in public or approved facts.

```text
Company profile
      ↓
Relevant approved insight
      ↓
Value-led introduction
      ↓
Content engagement
      ↓
Prioritized adviser follow-up
      ↓
Explicit owner intent
```

Engagement with content must not be treated as proof of willingness to sell.

### 7.3 Distribution priorities

- Nordic email-led nurture where legally and operationally appropriate.
- DACH call-first and relationship-led distribution.
- Adviser-led LinkedIn publishing and direct engagement.
- Small owner webinars, roundtables and partner events.
- Referral enablement for accountants, lawyers, tax advisers, wealth advisers and other trusted intermediaries.
- Local-language search pages for high-intent owner questions.
- Paid media mainly for narrow distribution and retargeting, not broad lead generation.

### 7.4 What not to build

- A generic AI blog generator.
- A full social-media scheduling product.
- A broad advertising platform.
- Automatically published company-specific financial analysis.
- A system that equates a click with transaction intent.
- Another MGX or generic M&A marketplace.

## 8. How substantial could the revenue effect be?

No credible public source found in this research provides a universal content-to-revenue conversion benchmark for lower-middle-market M&A advisers. The commercial impact must therefore be measured with Mergero's own funnel.

### 8.1 Required measurement model

Track:

```text
Eligible companies
→ reachable owners
→ content engagement
→ meaningful conversations
→ signed mandates
→ completed transactions
→ advisory revenue
```

Report sourced, influenced and assisted pipeline separately. Content may influence a mandate even when a referral, call or adviser receives the final attribution.

### 8.2 Illustrative sensitivity model

The following is hypothetical and is included only to show why small conversion changes may matter:

```text
2,000 targeted owners
× 5% meaningful-conversation rate
× 15% conversation-to-mandate rate
× 40% completion rate
= 6 completed transactions
```

If the meaningful-conversation rate increased from 5% to 6%, while every other assumption stayed constant:

```text
2,000 × 6% × 15% × 40% = 7.2 completed transactions
```

The modeled difference is 1.2 completed transactions, equivalent to `1.2 × average completion fee`.

At a purely hypothetical €200,000 average fee, that would equal €240,000. This figure is **not** a Mergero forecast and must not be presented externally as an expected result.

### 8.3 Data required from Mergero

Replace the assumptions with:

- Reachable owners per country.
- Conversation rate by channel.
- Content-assisted versus direct-outreach conversation rate.
- Conversation-to-mandate conversion.
- Mandate completion rate.
- Average retainer, progress and completion fees.
- Sales-cycle duration.
- Referral and repeat-client contribution.
- Adviser capacity and cost per qualified conversation.

## 9. Regional compliance evidence

This section provides source-backed risk boundaries, not legal advice. Mergero should have qualified counsel validate production policies by country and channel.

### 9.1 Germany

Section 7 of Germany's Act Against Unfair Competition states that:

- Commercial email generally requires the addressee's prior express consent, subject to the statute's existing-customer exception.
- A telephone call to another market participant requires at least presumed consent.
- Messages must identify the sender and provide a valid way to stop future communications.

**Source:** [German Act Against Unfair Competition, Section 7](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/englisch_uwg/englisch_uwg.html)

**Product implication:** DACH should remain call-first, permission- or relationship-led, with policy-controlled email follow-up.

### 9.2 Switzerland

The Swiss Federal Office of Communications states that automatic mass advertising by email, SMS, fax or telephone is permitted only when recipients have explicitly opted in.

**Source:** [Swiss Federal Office of Communications: When is mass sending allowed?](https://www.bakom.admin.ch/en/when-is-mass-sending-allowed)

**Product implication:** Do not treat a collected email address as authorization for automated mass marketing.

### 9.3 GDPR and legitimate interests

The European Commission states that direct marketing may sometimes rely on legitimate interest, but organizations must consider expectations and rights. When acquiring contact lists, the buyer must be able to demonstrate that data was obtained lawfully and may be used for advertising. Individuals must be informed and given the right to object.

The European Data Protection Board further states that direct marketing is not automatically justified by legitimate interest; purpose, necessity and balancing conditions must all be met.

**Sources:** [European Commission: Legal grounds for processing data](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/legal-grounds-processing-data_en), [EDPB Guidelines on legitimate interests](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202401_legitimateinterest_en.pdf)

**Product implication:** Store provenance, processing basis, notice status and objection/suppression state for personal contact data.

### 9.4 Finland

Finland's Competition and Consumer Authority states that electronic direct marketing to consumers usually requires advance consent and that recipients must be informed of their right to prohibit further messages. Business-to-business and named-person rules require separate legal analysis under the applicable electronic-communications and data-protection framework.

**Sources:** [Finnish Competition and Consumer Authority: Inappropriate direct marketing](https://www.kkv.fi/en/consumer-affairs/marketing-sales-and-indicating-prices/direct-marketing/inappropriate-direct-marketing/), [Finnish Data Protection Ombudsman B2B direct-marketing decision](https://www.finlex.fi/fi/viranomaiset/tietosuojavaltuutettu/2020/702)

**Product implication:** The Nordic email-first playbook must remain configurable, source-aware and reviewed rather than assuming every collected address is usable.

## 10. Testable hypotheses

The hackathon and any pilot should test rather than assume:

1. A value-led content introduction increases meaningful owner responses compared with a direct transaction question.
2. Buyer-demand content performs better than generic M&A commentary.
3. Company-specific asset selection outperforms one asset sent to every company.
4. Content engagement predicts a later conversation but does not reliably predict selling intent by itself.
5. DACH call briefs containing a relevant market insight improve connection-to-conversation conversion.
6. Referral partners generate higher-quality conversations than cold digital channels.
7. Automated nurture reduces the number of promising “not now” owners lost to manual follow-up.

Recommended experiment metrics:

- Delivery and valid-contact rate.
- Content view or download rate.
- Meaningful response rate.
- Qualified conversation rate.
- Future-interest rate.
- Mandates per 100 eligible companies.
- Time to qualified conversation.
- Opt-out and complaint rate.
- Completed transactions and realized revenue over the longer term.

## 11. Bottom line for the team

The marketing layer is justified because Mergero operates in a high-trust category with infrequent buying occasions and high revenue per successful mandate. Outreach captures owners who are ready or responsive now. Evidence-led content helps Mergero earn recognition and trust among owners who are not ready, supports referrals, and makes later outreach more credible.

The product proposition is therefore:

> Continuously build the company universe, turn proprietary market intelligence into useful owner-facing content, use engagement to improve timing and prioritization, and automate the appropriate path from first contact to a qualified Mergero conversation.

Marketing is not an adjacent feature. It is the long-term activation and nurture layer around the same company intelligence used for direct outreach and MGX qualification.
